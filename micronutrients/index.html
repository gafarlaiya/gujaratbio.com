Micronutrients in agriculture: what they are, how they work and why they matter

Micronutrients and trace elements for farmers: the full list, what each does, deficiency signs, fertilizer types, and how to diagnose and correct problems.

Micronutrients are the nutrients a crop needs in very small amounts: zinc, iron, manganese, copper, boron, molybdenum, chlorine and nickel. They switch on the enzymes, hormones and processes that let the crop use nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, form chlorophyll, flower, set fruit and resist stress. A shortage of any one limits the whole crop, however much NPK is applied, and Indian soil tests show such shortages are widespread.

By Gafar Laiya and the Gujarat Bio Organics team. Updated November 2026.

What are micronutrients?

Plants need 17 essential nutrients. Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen come from air and water. The rest come from the soil and fall into groups by how much the crop needs. Micronutrients, also called trace elements, are needed in grams per acre rather than kilograms, but a crop cannot complete its life without them.

GroupNutrientsNeeded in
Major nutrientsNitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K)Large amounts
Secondary nutrientsCalcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), sulphur (S)Moderate amounts
Micronutrients (trace elements)zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), manganese (Mn), copper (Cu), boron (B), molybdenum (Mo), chlorine (Cl), nickel (Ni)Very small amounts
Beneficial elementsSilicon (Si), cobalt (Co), sodium (Na), selenium (Se)Useful for some crops or for the microbes and animals that depend on them

How micronutrients work

  • They switch on enzymes: zinc, manganese, copper, iron, molybdenum and nickel are parts of, or activators for, enzymes that drive growth and energy.
  • They make chlorophyll possible: iron and manganese are needed to form it and run photosynthesis; magnesium sits at its centre.
  • They make hormones: zinc is needed for auxin, the growth hormone.
  • They build cell walls: boron and calcium hold walls together, and silicon strengthens them.
  • They make flowers fertile: boron, zinc and copper are needed for pollen and seed set.
  • They help use nitrogen: molybdenum, nickel and sulphur turn nitrogen into protein; molybdenum and cobalt help legumes fix it.
  • They defend the crop: copper, manganese, boron and silicon strengthen tissue against pests and disease.

India's soils: shortages are widespread

An ICAR survey of 2,42,827 soil samples from 615 districts found 58.6% of soils short of sulphur, 51.2% of zinc, 44.7% of boron, 19.2% of iron, 17.4% of manganese and 11.4% of copper, counting hidden shortages. Many fields were short of two or three nutrients together, most often sulphur with zinc, zinc with boron and sulphur with boron. Alkaline and calcareous soils, low organic matter and years of urea, DAP and MOP without sulphur or micronutrients are the main reasons.

Indian soil figures: Shukla AK, Behera SK and others (2021). Deficiency of phyto-available sulphur, zinc, boron, iron, copper and manganese in soils of India. Scientific Reports 11, 19760. Based on 2,42,827 soil samples from 615 districts in 28 states. Read the study. The figures include soils with a hidden (latent) shortage.

Common practices that miss the cause

  • Applying more urea when leaves turn yellow, though yellowing from sulphur, iron, zinc or magnesium does not respond to nitrogen.
  • Using NPK water-soluble fertilizer as a cure-all, though most grades carry little or no micronutrient.
  • Spraying a pesticide when the crop looks weak, even when no pest is present.
  • Treating the nutrient that shows the symptom, when the real limit is another nutrient, the soil pH or the water supply.

How to identify and solve micronutrient problems

  • Read the leaves: old or young, pattern and colour.
  • Rule out pests, disease, water and weather.
  • Test the soil before the season and the leaves in orchards.
  • Fix the cause first: pH, drainage, water supply and fertilizer balance.
  • Correct the short nutrient by soil, foliar or seed treatment, at the right stage and the right dose.
  • Build soil organic matter so the shortage does not return.
  • Follow the step-by-step guide.

Questions

What are micronutrients in agriculture?

Nutrients a crop needs in very small amounts: zinc, iron, manganese, copper, boron, molybdenum, chlorine and nickel. Calcium, magnesium and sulphur are secondary nutrients, and silicon and cobalt are beneficial elements.

What is the difference between micronutrients and trace elements?

In farming the two terms are used for the same group: nutrients needed only in trace amounts. Trace elements is sometimes also used for beneficial elements such as cobalt and selenium.

Which micronutrient is most deficient in Indian soils?

Among micronutrients, zinc, followed by boron. Sulphur, a secondary nutrient, is even more widely short.

Can NPK alone give a good crop?

Only if the soil supplies enough of every other nutrient. Where a micronutrient is short, more NPK does not lift the yield.

How do I know which micronutrient my crop needs?

Read the leaf symptoms, rule out pests and disease, and confirm with a soil and leaf test.
