15	Apple leaf fall: what to do before the tree goes dormant
16	Apple growers in Himachal, Kashmir and Uttarakhand: what to do from yellow leaves to bare tree. Urea, fallen leaves, trunk, root checks and pruning.
17	Before apple leaves fall, decide on one regional urea spray only if more than 70% of leaves are still on. Once leaves drop, collect or treat them to lower scab next spring, clean the trunk and basin, mark weak trees for a root check, and keep pruning for the dormant season.
18	Updated 2026-11-09
19	See also: <a href="/crops/apple">Apple guide</a> and <a href="/products/immunity-apple-special">Immunity Apple Special</a>.
20	Leaf fall changes with altitude, variety and the weather that year, so go by your own trees and not by the date. Former Nauni professor SP Bhardwaj writes that <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/hill-view/chilling-hours-and-why-these-matter-for-fruit-growers/amp/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">deciduous trees stop growing below 12°C</a>, and then the leaves yellow and drop. This page covers only that short period. For the full year, see the <a href="/crops/apple">apple guide</a>.

34	While the leaves are on: one nutrition decision
35	Advice differs by region, so use the figure for your area.
36	<strong>Himachal Pradesh:</strong> Bhardwaj says <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/after-harvest-science-of-growing-better-apples-in-himachal" rel="noopener" target="_blank">nutrient sprays should go only on orchards with more than 70% of their leaves</a>, and gives urea at 2% (4 kg in 200 litres of water).
37	<strong>Kashmir:</strong> a SKUAST-K plant pathologist writes that <a href="https://kashmirreader.com/2021/10/18/clearing-misunderstandings-vis-a-vis-post-harvest-urea-application-in-apple/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">the university recommended 3 to 4% urea</a>, with 3% for trees with heavy growth. The article says the best time is just before natural leaf fall, in early November.
38	<strong>Uttarakhand:</strong> we did not find a published figure for this spray. Ask your KVK before using one.
39	The same Kashmir article warns against two mistakes. Spraying in early or mid-October can push more growth and delay hardening. And growers in south Kashmir reported bud damage and crop loss from 5% urea on the tree.
40	Keep your hopes for this spray modest. In <a href="https://dissem.in/p/72670051/leaf-absorption-withdrawal-and-remobilization-of-autumn-applied-urea-n-in-apple" rel="noopener" target="_blank">one Canadian trial on Jonagold</a>, leaves took up about 19% of the urea that landed on them in three days, in cold weather near 5°C. The stored nitrogen supplied only 3.8% of flower bud nitrogen and did not change flower quality or fruit set. In <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8202005" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a Tasmanian trial on Gala</a>, nitrogen given through drip after harvest was taken up less well than nitrogen given before harvest. Those were other countries and other methods, but both suggest this spray cannot make up for a season of poor feeding.
41	November is also a good month to test soil. Bhardwaj advises a <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/after-harvest-science-of-growing-better-apples-in-himachal" rel="noopener" target="_blank">soil test once in four to five years, in October or November</a>. Manure is not urgent. In a <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/60529/24788/151746" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CITH trial at Mukteshwar</a> it went on in the last week of December, in a <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/79606/33366/354610" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Kinnaur trial</a> in January, and the <a href="https://horticulture.jk.gov.in/FAQs.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">J&amp;K Horticulture Department</a> gives February to March as the best time in its region.
42	After the leaves drop: the scab job
43	The scab fungus <a href="https://eudyan.hp.gov.in/cms/media/qadipm3c/spray-schedule-of-apple-english.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">survives the winter on fallen leaves</a>. In Kashmir, its spores cause first infection around green tip in <a href="https://kashmirreader.com/2021/10/18/clearing-misunderstandings-vis-a-vis-post-harvest-urea-application-in-apple/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">mid-March</a>. The leaves you leave on the ground in November can start next year's scab.
44	The Himachal schedule for 2023 gives two choices: collect the leaves and rot them in a compost pit, or spray 5% urea on the fallen leaves on the orchard floor so they break down fast. The 5% is for leaves on the ground, never for the tree.
45	The research is mixed, and it helps to know this. A <a href="https://apsnet.org/publications/plantdisease/2000/December/Pages/84_12_1319.aspx" rel="noopener" target="_blank">New Hampshire study</a> reported that urea in November, with about 95% of leaves down, cut trapped spores by 50%. Shredding all the litter could cut scab risk by 80 to 90%, but only 50 to 65% when part of the litter was missed. A <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fhort.2023.1175168" rel="noopener" target="_blank">South African paper</a> notes urea had no effect on scab there. So do not count on urea alone. On narrow terraces where a machine cannot go, raking is the practical way to get the leaves out.
46	Also clear <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/nauni-varsity-lists-dos-and-donts-on-apple-leaf-disease-640572" rel="noopener" target="_blank">weeds and infected plant debris</a>, mummified fruit and dead twigs. Bhardwaj suggests burying leaves near the drip basin under thin soil. If scab was bad this year, the compost pit is the safer choice. Either way, keep leaves and soil away from the <a href="/crop-problems/apple-root-rot">collar of the tree</a>.
47	Trunk and basin
48	Whitewashing is <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/after-harvest-science-of-growing-better-apples-in-himachal" rel="noopener" target="_blank">advised in Himachal</a> to protect against temperature swings and insects. The J&K department's <a href="https://horticulture.jk.gov.in/FAQs.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAQ</a> lists it for peach, plum and apricot, but not apple. So in Kashmir, treat it as optional. It may matter most on young trunks. A <a href="https://fruitscout.mgcafe.uky.edu/sunscald-injury-lower-trunk-apple" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Kentucky guide</a> explains why young trunks are at risk: winter sun warms the bark, mainly on the south-west side, and a sudden drop in temperature then splits it.
49	Keep the basin clean, and let water run away from the trunk. Do not flood it. Too much water <a href="https://extension.umaine.edu/fruit/?p=842" rel="noopener" target="_blank">limits how hardy a tree can become</a>. If a long dry spell comes with cold, evening surface irrigation was <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/guidelines-issued-to-protect-orchards-from-winter-dry-spell/amp" rel="noopener" target="_blank">suggested by the Nurpur station</a> for its area.
50	Mark weak trees before the leaves go
51	Leaf fall is the easiest time to compare trees. A tree that yellowed and dropped leaves well before its neighbours deserves a look. Early leaf fall has other causes too. The Himachal schedule also lists premature leaf fall among leaf disease problems, so check the roots before deciding.
52	Tie a cloth on the tree now. Later, open the soil and look at the roots. In a <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/98530/39129/341426" rel="noopener" target="_blank">study from the Kumaon hills</a>, white root rot turned lateral roots dark brown and covered them with a white fungal mat. Top symptoms appeared only at a very advanced stage, and adult trees survived two to three seasons. A review lists <a href="https://www.microbiologyjournal.org/biology-and-control-of-rosellinia-necatrix-causing-white-root-rot-disease-a-review/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">yellowing and falling leaves</a>, then wilting and death.
53	Timing matters here. The <a href="https://eudyan.hp.gov.in/cms/media/qadipm3c/spray-schedule-of-apple-english.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Himachal schedule</a> places basin drenching for white root rot and collar rot in the rainy season. Winter is for opening the soil, exposing infected roots to the sun, cutting out infected parts and painting collar wounds. Take the product from the current year's schedule of your state agricultural university or KVK.
54	<a href="/products/immunity-apple-special">Immunity Apple Special</a>, made at our own plant at GIDC Shihor, Bhavnagar, is made for apple orchards with root problems and low production. It is not a fungicide and does not replace these checks. If you use it, follow the dose on the pack.
55	What not to do before dormancy
56	Do not spray urea in early or mid-October, or at 5% on the tree.
57	Do not give nutrient sprays to a tree that has already lost most of its leaves.
58	Do not drench basins in November because you are worried about root rot.
59	Do not <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/nauni-varsity-lists-dos-and-donts-on-apple-leaf-disease-640572" rel="noopener" target="_blank">mix fungicides with insecticides or nutrients</a> unless the mix is approved.
60	Do not start the main pruning.
61	When pruning should wait
62	Bhardwaj writes that <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/hill-view/pruning-precision-secret-to-healthier-high-yield-apple-orchards" rel="noopener" target="_blank">November and December pruning leaves cuts exposed for too long</a>, and gives February to early March as the best window. The same column warns against pruning in freezing conditions and against removing more than 20 to 25% of the crown in one season. The <a href="https://horticulture.jk.gov.in/FAQs.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">J&amp;K department</a> gives December to February for temperate fruits generally. Neither gives a November date.
63	There is one exception. When snow comes with leaves still on, broken limbs need attention. Before forecast snow in November 2019, a <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/govt-asks-orchardists-to-prune-apple-trees-860367" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SKUAST advisory in Kashmir</a> told growers to reduce canopy load by pruning. Cut the damage, not the whole tree, and clean your tools.

87	Does urea make apple leaves fall?
88	The SKUAST-K pathologist writing in Kashmir Reader says urea on its own does not cause defoliation in apple. Some growers spray right after harvest hoping for early leaf drop, and the article says this can prolong growth instead.
89	Does an apple tree need snow for chilling?
90	Chilling is counted from air temperature, not from ice. Bhardwaj gives 0 to 7.2°C as the most effective range, and 800 to 1,200 chill units for apple generally, with less for Golden Delicious and Gala types.
91	When should I take leaf samples for testing?
92	Bhardwaj advises collecting leaf samples in the second fortnight of August every year. If you missed it this year, plan for next August and use the soil test for now.
93	Do organic drenches work on white root rot?
94	In one Himachal Pradesh study on pot and nursery plants, a cow urine decoction gave good disease reduction. That work was not on grown orchard trees, and results in an orchard may differ. Ask your KVK before relying on it.
95	Is any rootstock safe from white root rot?
96	A 2020 study from the Kumaon hills found none of the available apple rootstocks to be immune. The authors recommended Paron, a Malus baccata type, as a resistant option. Ask your local nursery or KVK when replanting.

102	Ascophyllum, Sargassum, Kappaphycus: what is the difference?
103	Brown or red, farmed or wild: how these three seaweeds differ, what studies report, and how a farmer should choose for crop, stage and purpose.
104	Ascophyllum and Sargassum are brown seaweeds. Kappaphycus is a red seaweed. They grow in different places and contain different main sugars: alginate and fucoidan in brown, carrageenan in red. Studies report benefits from all three in different crops, so no species is best on paper. Choose by crop, stage, purpose, extraction and your own trial.
105	See also: <a href="/plant-science-series">Plant Science Series</a> and <a href="/products">the Immunity range</a>.
106	A label may say Ascophyllum, Sargassum or Kappaphycus, and a seller may tell you his species is the one to buy. This page sets out what is really known about the three, and how to use that when you choose.
107	Brown seaweed or red seaweed
108	Ascophyllum and Sargassum are brown seaweeds. Kappaphycus is a red seaweed. The group matters because it decides the main sugars in the plant. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">One review</a> reports that brown seaweeds contain 17 to 45% alginates and 5 to 20% fucoidan, along with laminarin. Red seaweeds contain carrageenans instead. In India, Kappaphycus is farmed mainly <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/108193/42691" rel="noopener" target="_blank">as a source of kappa carrageenan</a>.
109	The same review says the type and amount of these sugars depend on the species and on where it grew, and change with the season. So two batches of one species can also differ.

135	What studies report for each
136	<strong>Ascophyllum.</strong> <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">A review of Ascophyllum products</a> lists alginic acid at 28%, fucoidans at 11.6%, mannitol at 7.5% and laminarin at 4.5%, and reports betaines such as glycine betaine in the extract. It reports that foliar sprays improved fruit quality in watermelon, apple, olive and grape. It also reports less drought stress in soybean and bean, and better salt tolerance in tomato.
137	<strong>Sargassum.</strong> <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">A wider review</a> lists Sargassum extracts among those that increased tolerance to salt and drought in tomato and sweet pepper. In India, the most useful study for a Gujarat reader was done at CSMCRI, Bhavnagar, and is described below.
138	<strong>Kappaphycus.</strong> The same review describes improved drought and salt tolerance in wheat and drought tolerance in soybean. In a <a href="https://pub.isa-india.in/index.php/ija/article/download/4473/4148" rel="noopener" target="_blank">two-season rice trial at Ranchi, Jharkhand</a>, the crop sprayed with Kappaphycus sap gave more grains per panicle, and the most productive treatment also received the full recommended fertiliser dose. In a <a href="https://pub.isa-india.in/index.php/ija/article/download/4374/4049/4569" rel="noopener" target="_blank">maize trial in Meghalaya</a>, Kappaphycus sap and sap of another red seaweed, Gracilaria, performed at a similar level as foliar sprays.
139	These are results for particular extracts, crops and places. They do not tell you what any product on the shop shelf will do on your farm.
140	Why no species wins on paper
141	The studies to look at are the ones that test species together, in one crop, in one place. Two of them show the problem.
142	In a <a href="https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2023.1273355/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">pot study at CSMCRI, Bhavnagar</a>, maize was sprayed with simple Kappaphycus and Sargassum preparations, alone and in blends. Sargassum alone gave the highest seed yield at the lower dose, but Kappaphycus alone at the lower dose was on par with it. Some blends were comparable. The best response was generally at the lower dose, and the highest Sargassum dose was on par with no spray at all. This was one hybrid in one rabi season in a net house, so it is not a rule for field crops.
143	In a <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/2673-9410/5/2/22" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2025 tomato trial</a>, Sargassum extract gave the highest fruit Brix and Ascophyllum extract the highest proline, a compound linked to plant stress response. A commercial Ascophyllum extract showed lower proline than the other preparations. So one species gave different results depending on how it was prepared.
144	Extraction can change what you are buying
145	<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">The Ascophyllum review</a> says composition and activity depend on the extraction method. Alkaline extraction breaks long sugars into smaller pieces and can produce compounds that were not in the seaweed. Acid treatment removes some phenolic compounds. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Another review</a> says alkaline extraction at high pressure is the most widely used process and has been found effective, although some hormone-like molecules can be degraded.

188	How much seed, what spacing and when to water cumin at sowing?
189	Cumin seed rate, row spacing, November sowing dates, seed treatment, the first two irrigations and the sowing mistakes that can lead to wilt later.
190	ICAR-NRCSS guidance gives cumin 10 to 12 kg seed per hectare, in rows 25 cm apart, sown no deeper than 1.5 cm. Sow in the first week of November in Gujarat and 15 to 30 November in Rajasthan. Irrigate lightly just after sowing, then again 8 to 10 days after sowing.
191	See also: <a href="/crops/cumin">Cumin (Jeera) guide</a> and <a href="/products/immunity-cumin-special">Immunity Cumin Special</a>.
192	Cumin gives you few chances to correct a bad start. The field, the seed, the sowing depth and the first two waterings are all decided in November, and several wilt risks are set at this stage. We have worked with farmers since 2006, and patchy fields and dying plants are what cumin growers in Gujarat and Rajasthan bring to us. Our <a href="/crops/cumin">cumin crop guide</a> covers the whole season. This page covers only sowing day to the second irrigation.
193	Seed rate, spacing and sowing date
194	Figures differ by institute, region and sowing method.

200	10 to 12 kg per hectare

211	Sources: <a href="https://actascientific.com/ASAG/pdf/ASAG-02-0052.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR-NRCSS</a>, <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/151830/64080/504816" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SDAU Jagudan</a> and <a href="https://eagriorg.github.io/eagri50/HORT282/lec06.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a>. The <a href="https://www.Indianspices.Com/sites/default/files/cultivation_practices-CUMIN-1.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Spices Board</a> also gives 10 to 12 kg per hectare, 25 cm by 10 cm and a 1.5 cm depth limit.
212	<strong>Seed.</strong> 10 to 12 kg per hectare is about 4 to 5 kg per acre. Do not add extra seed just for safety. The <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/151830/64080/504816" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Jagudan authors</a> call cumin sensitive to plant density.
213	<strong>Depth.</strong> NRCSS and the Spices Board both say not deeper than 1.5 cm.
214	<strong>Spacing.</strong> At <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/1309/929" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NRCSS Ajmer</a>, a trial with variety RZ-209 tested rows at 20, 25 and 30 cm, and 25 cm by 10 cm gave the best seed yield and return. At <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/151830/64080/504816" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Jagudan</a>, on loamy sand, 30 cm rows gave more than 15 cm rows or broadcasting. Rows of 25 to 30 cm are the range to work in. Your local agricultural university or KVK can say which suits your soil.
215	<strong>Date.</strong> In the three-season Jagudan trial, cumin sown on 5 November gave 497 kg per hectare, and cumin sown on 20 November gave 344 kg. The authors link later sowing to a shorter growing period. That is one trial on one soil, and your result will differ. But if your field is ready, do not delay without a reason. A <a href="https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/85076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review of cumin production</a> also says the date should keep flowering away from humid weather.
216	Seed treatment before sowing
217	Use seed from a field that had no wilt. <a href="https://eagriorg.github.io/eagri50/HORT282/lec06.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a> says seed should come only from disease-free plots.
218	<strong>Biological:</strong> the <a href="https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/85076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review</a> gives Trichoderma culture at 10 g per kg of seed.
219	<strong>Chemical:</strong> seed dressings are also listed in these guides. Follow the current recommendation of your state agricultural university or KVK, and check the label before mixing any chemical with a living culture.
220	<strong>Soaking:</strong> the same review says priming the seed for 8 hours and drying it in shade improves germination. TNAU also says soaked seed should be dried in shade before broadcasting.
221	First irrigation: light, and straight after sowing
222	<a href="https://actascientific.com/ASAG/pdf/ASAG-02-0052.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR-NRCSS</a> and <a href="https://eagriorg.github.io/eagri50/HORT282/lec06.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a> both say the first irrigation goes on just after sowing, and TNAU says it should be light. The seed sits only about 1.5 cm deep, so a heavy flow can move it. The <a href="https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/85076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review</a> warns against heavy irrigation at this stage for exactly this reason.
223	Make small beds. TNAU describes beds of 4 m by 3 m with irrigation channels.
224	Level each bed first, so water does not run to one corner.
225	Let water enter slowly, and cut it off before the bed floods.
226	Do not let water stand on the field.
227	Seed heaped at the end of a bed, or bare strips at the entry point, can mean the flow was too strong.
228	Second irrigation: 8 to 10 days after sowing
229	NRCSS says the second irrigation should be given during germination, 8 to 10 days after sowing. TNAU gives 6 to 10 days after the first irrigation. Soil and weather decide where you fall in that range. The review notes that seedlings often show after 10 to 12 days, typically after this second watering, so do not panic and flood the field on day 7.
230	If a crust forms
231	A crust is a hard, cement-like skin on the soil surface. The crop guides we read for cumin do not set a rule for it, so this advice comes from <a href="https://blog-fruit-vegetable-ipm.extension.umn.edu/2020/06/managing-soil-crusting.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">University of Minnesota Extension</a>, written for vegetable crops. It says crust is most likely after heavy rain followed by hot, dry weather, and in clay, silt and loam soils. We would treat a heavy irrigation the same way. If seedlings are about to come up, it suggests irrigating to soften the surface.
232	For cumin, that means giving the second irrigation lightly and on time. Take care not to cut young seedlings with a tool while they are coming through. The <a href="https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/85076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review</a> notes that later weedings also break crust. The first weeding and hoeing is about 30 to 40 days after sowing, according to TNAU. More organic matter in the soil can help prevent crusting, which is one more reason to <a href="/knowledge/soil-health">build soil health</a> before the next season.
233	After the second irrigation, do not water by habit. NRCSS gives 4 to 6 irrigations in all, 20 to 25 days apart, depending on soil. In one <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/1309/929" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NRCSS Ajmer trial</a>, four irrigations at 18-day gaps gave more seed than six at 12-day gaps. Cumin is <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/19374/1/IJSS%20R.Singh.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">highly sensitive to water stagnation</a>.
234	Sowing mistakes that can show up later as wilt
235	Wilt is caused by a <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/168583/62702/491018" rel="noopener" target="_blank">soil-borne fungus</a>, Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cumini. Tips and leaves droop, and young plants are hit hardest. The <a href="https://www.Indianspices.Com/sites/default/files/cultivation_practices-CUMIN-1.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Spices Board</a> says efficient control is not possible with chemicals, so what you do before and at sowing matters.
236	<strong>Same field again.</strong> NRCSS and the Spices Board advise a three-year rotation. The Spices Board reports less wilt after non-host crops such as mustard and pearl millet. A <a href="https://updatepublishing.com/journal/index.php/josac/article/view/4925" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Junagadh study</a> found one kharif crop of sorghum or maize did not reduce wilt on its own.
237	<strong>Saving seed from a wilted patch.</strong> TNAU, NRCSS and the Spices Board all ask for healthy, disease-free seed.
238	<strong>Ignoring the variety.</strong> The Spices Board lists RZ-223 and GC-4 as tolerant to Fusarium wilt. Tolerant does not mean immune. Check what is advised for your region.
239	<strong>Broadcasting.</strong> The <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/151830/64080/504816" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Jagudan authors</a> warn it may lead to severe disease and pest attack.
240	<strong>Water standing in low spots.</strong> The <a href="https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/85076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review</a> says stagnation and excess moisture are harmful. Drain or level these patches.
241	<strong>Ignoring nematodes.</strong> The Spices Board says wilt is worse when root-knot nematode attacks before the wilt fungus.
242	Soil work can also help. In a two-season <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/180288/67746/549983" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Jagudan trial</a>, neem cake at 2 t per hectare before sowing had 13.54% wilt, against 38.51% in untreated plots. In a <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/180290/68157/558056" rel="noopener" target="_blank">second Jagudan trial</a>, farmyard manure enriched with Trichoderma harzianum had 11.46% against 39.41%. In the Junagadh study, covering the soil with thin plastic for 15 days in summer cut wilt to 26.27% from 44.90%. These results may not repeat in your field, and solarisation is a job for next summer.

267	Frost can burn potato and cumin in one night: what to do
268	<a href="/">Home</a> / <a href="/articles">Articles</a> / Frost can burn potato and cumin in one night...
269	Frost can burn potato and cumin in one night: what to do
270	On a clear, calm winter evening after a frost warning, give a light irrigation before night. Cover young plants with thatch or gunny bags. Avoid deep irrigation and extra fertiliser. Frost can freeze plant cells in potato, cumin and young orchards. Next morning, check the damage before you plough or prune anything.
271	See also: <a href="/crops/potato">Potato guide</a> and <a href="/products/immunity-potato-special">Immunity Potato Special</a>.
272	Why one cold night can matter
273	Frost is not only a hill problem. In December 2021, a <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/68377/1/CWR-16-12-2021.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">national crop weather bulletin</a> carried a warning of ground frost in isolated pockets of Saurashtra and Kutch, and of Punjab. In December 2025, <a href="https://mausam.imd.gov.in/Forecast/mcmarq/mcmarq_data/Impact_Based_Forecast_25.12.2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IMD's Jaipur centre</a>, in its table for Sikar district, listed "freezing of plant cells due to frost" as the risk for cumin and for newly developed orchards.
274	The three crops in this guide are at risk at different times:
275	<strong>Potato:</strong> ICAR-CPRI's <a href="https://cpri.icar.gov.in/AddLink/getIMG/6165" rel="noopener" target="_blank">variety bulletin</a> names frost in the later stages of the autumn crop as a concern in the north-western and north-central plains. In the severe 2002-03 cold wave, <a href="https://www.un-spider.org/sites/default/files/6-Cold%20Wave_Impacts_India_Indian%20Council%20of%20Agricultural%20Research.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR's report</a> recorded a 25% potato loss around Agra. That was one bad year in one area, but it shows the size of the risk.
276	<strong>Cumin:</strong> ICAR scientists at Jodhpur <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJSS/article/download/175247/66515/532833" rel="noopener" target="_blank">write</a> that frost can cause severe injury at flowering and fruiting. On our <a href="/crops/cumin">cumin page</a>, that stage is about 6 to 10 weeks after sowing.
277	<strong>Young orchards:</strong> the same ICAR report found more damage in younger plants than in older ones, and more in low-lying parts of the field.
278	How to tell a frost night is coming
279	Use the forecast first, then look at your own field in the evening.
280	<strong>Check the warning.</strong> The <a href="https://moes.gov.in/sites/default/files/PIB2110767.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Meghdoot and Mausam apps</a> give district weather alerts. Agromet units issue district advisories every Tuesday and Friday, and these also go out by SMS. Look for the words "ground frost" or "cold wave".
281	<strong>Check again on the day.</strong> <a href="https://mausam.imd.gov.in/backend/assets/aiwfb_pdf/84d387d0624c1efa29c356515e6f5964.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IMD says</a> forecast accuracy falls as the lead time grows. A warning made five days ahead can change.
282	<strong>Look at the sky and feel the wind.</strong> ICAR's report says radiation frost occurs on calm, clear nights. Very low humidity and no rain add to the risk.
283	<strong>Read the dew point, if your app shows it.</strong> ICAR's report quotes a rule from Victoria, Australia: frost is possible when the dew point is below 6°C and probable below 0°C. This is not an Indian rule, so use it only as a guide.
284	<strong>Know your cold corner.</strong> <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e08.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAO's frost manual</a> says low spots are colder and get more damage, and that forecasts for a single farm are usually not available. Your low corner can freeze when the rest of the field does not.
285	Before dark: give water, but lightly
286	Water comes up again and again in these sources.
287	<a href="https://mausam.imd.gov.in/Forecast/mcmarq/mcmarq_data/Impact_Based_Forecast_25.12.2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IMD Jaipur</a> says a light irrigation before night keeps the field surface warm and lowers the chance of frost. It also says not to irrigate very deeply.
288	<a href="https://www.un-spider.org/sites/default/files/6-Cold%20Wave_Impacts_India_Indian%20Council%20of%20Agricultural%20Research.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR's report</a> advises light, frequent irrigations in December and January where water is available.
289	The <a href="https://www.icar-crida.res.in/CP/Gujarat/SKDAU,Banasakantha/GUJ%2022-Ahmedabad%20%2004.105.2011.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">contingency plan for Ahmedabad district</a> gives similar cold wave advice: frequent irrigation with a low depth of water.
290	For potato, an <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/view/49947" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Indian Farming article</a> reports that furrow irrigation can prevent frost damage to a large extent.
291	<a href="https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e08.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAO</a> explains why this works. Dry soil has more air spaces and stores less heat. It says to wet dry soil well before the frost, so the sun can warm it. So irrigate in the daytime if you can, not at the last minute.
292	<strong>Cumin needs extra care.</strong> It does not like wet soil, and an <a href="https://www.Indianspices.Com/sites/default/files/cultivation_practices-CUMIN-1.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR guide</a> says its blight is favoured by humid, cloudy weather. Keep the water light, and irrigate for a real frost warning, not for every cold evening.
293	<strong>Sprinklers are a special case.</strong> FAO says that once sprinklers are started on a frost night, they should run without a break, and hand-moved sets should not be stopped and moved. Too little water in windy weather can do more harm than no sprinkler at all. If you cannot keep a set running all night, do not start it at night. IMD Jaipur's advice is different: a light sprinkle as the sun rises, to melt the frost.
294	Young orchards: covers and moist roots
295	<a href="https://raubikaner.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/BE-10.01.2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SKRAU Bikaner's advisories</a>, written for Bikaner district, say to cover new plants with munja thatch, gunny bags or other local material that is not wool.
296	ICAR's report says to use plastic or straw thatch for nurseries and young fruit plants, and to leave the south-eastern side open for sunlight.
297	<a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/punjab-agricultural-university-experts-give-tips-to-farmers-to-counter-frost-473408" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PAU experts, as reported by The Tribune</a>, advise keeping the soil around the root zone wet, because moist soil stays warmer.
298	For the long term, ICAR advises windbreaks around orchards in cold-wave areas.
299	The sources disagree on mulch. ICAR says organic mulch insulates the soil. FAO says vegetative mulch keeps heat out of the soil and can make orchard crops more frost prone after bud break. PAU's advice to use mulch is for soft vegetables. In orchards at risk, ask your KVK before you lay a thick mulch under the trees. Our <a href="/crops/fruits">fruit orchards page</a> has more on orchard care.
300	Smoke and sprays: read this first
301	Smoke is listed in Indian advisories. <a href="https://mausam.imd.gov.in/Forecast/mcmarq/mcmarq_data/Impact_Based_Forecast_25.12.2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IMD Jaipur</a> says to make a slow smoke of dry grass or waste on the field edges before sunrise. ICAR's report and the Ahmedabad plan also mention it. But <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e08.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAO</a> says plainly that smoke does not help, pollutes the air and should be avoided. So do not depend on smoke. Do the water and the covers first. If you still use smoke, keep the fire small and watched, away from dry crop.
302	Some Rajasthan advisories also list a 0.1% sulphuric acid spray: <a href="https://raubikaner.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/BE-28.01.2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SKRAU Bikaner</a> for field crops and orchards in Bikaner district, and IMD Jaipur's table for Sikar district, which includes cumin and new orchards. Potato is not on that list. This is an acid. Do not mix it from memory. Use it only if your KVK advises it for your crop and tells you how to handle it safely.
303	The morning after
304	<strong>As the sun rises,</strong> IMD Jaipur advises a light sprinkle of water, if you can, to melt the frost.
305	<strong>Do not plough the crop in a hurry.</strong> In <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/PotatoJ/article/view/32441" rel="noopener" target="_blank">one potato trial</a>, a 60-day crop lost 60 to 70% of its leaves to frost, and the plots that got no spray still gave tubers. Sprayed plots gave more, but that is one trial, not a recipe. Your crop may do better or worse.
306	<strong>Look at the damage.</strong> ICAR's report describes soaked leaves or soft spots on chilled plants. In fruit trees it describes killed foliage, rolled leaves, dried shoots and cracked bark.
307	<strong>Do not add extra fertiliser.</strong> IMD Jaipur says not to use too much. FAO says nitrogen and phosphorus before a frost encourage growth and can make plants more easily damaged.
308	<strong>Do not prune orchards now.</strong> <a href="https://www.un-spider.org/sites/default/files/6-Cold%20Wave_Impacts_India_Indian%20Council%20of%20Agricultural%20Research.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ICAR's report</a> says to prune frost-hit parts at the end of February or early March, then irrigate soon after, if water is available. Ask your KVK how to protect the cut ends, and test the soil before you feed.
309	<strong>Watch cumin for blight.</strong> SKRAU Bikaner warns of blight when the sky is cloudy. Follow the current recommendation of your state agricultural university or KVK for any spray.
310	<strong>Check the forecast again.</strong> Frost can return the next night, so keep the plan ready.
311	If your potato plot is hit often, note it and plan for next season. CPRI lists frost tolerance as a need for the plains, and your KVK can tell you which varieties suit your region. Our <a href="/crops/potato">potato page</a> covers the rest of the crop's care.

329	Is a cold wave the same as frost?
330	No. ICAR's report describes frost as the temperature of the ground surface and objects on it falling below freezing. A cold wave is measured by how far the minimum temperature falls below normal. You can have a cold wave without frost.
331	Will light irrigation fully protect my crop?
332	No method is a guarantee. FAO reports up to 3 to 4°C of protection from flood irrigation in low tree and vine crops, when it is done before the frost. That figure is not for potato or cumin, and a very cold night can still cause damage.
333	Is mango also at risk?
334	Yes. In the 2002-03 cold wave, ICAR's report ranked mango as the most injured among the fruit crops it listed, ahead of papaya, banana, litchi, pomegranate and amla. Damage varied by age and place in the field.
335	Should I spray a growth regulator after frost?
336	One potato trial reported better regrowth with a growth regulator spray a week after frost. ICAR's cold wave report also says such chemicals may help. Treat this as research. Ask your KVK before you spray anything on a frosted crop.

337	Grapes after October pruning: the first 45 days
338	Stage-by-stage guide for October-pruned grapes in Maharashtra and Karnataka: shoot count, thinning, water, feeding, downy mildew and bunch checks.
339	<a href="/">Home</a> / <a href="/articles">Articles</a> / Grapes after October pruning: what to do in the...
340	Grapes after October pruning: what to do in the first 45 days
341	Stage-by-stage guide for October-pruned grapes in Maharashtra and Karnataka: shoot count, thinning, water, feeding, downy mildew and bunch checks.
342	In the first 45 days, count sprouted shoots by day 15, thin at the 4 to 5 leaf stage, and feed nitrogen and micronutrients by stage. Cut irrigation sharply at bloom. Scout for downy mildew after every rain or heavy dew, and keep only the number of bunches your market needs.
343	See also: <a href="/crops">Crop guides</a> and <a href="/products">the Immunity range</a>.
344	First find your day number. Count from your pruning date. A vine pruned on 1 October is at about day 39 on 9 November. A vine pruned on 25 October is at about day 15.
345	The days below come from the <a href="https://nrcgrapes.icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/publications/Calendar%20of%20Activities%20during%20different%20growth%20stages%20of%20grapevine%20-Fruit%20Pruning.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">fruit pruning calendar of ICAR-National Research Centre for Grapes, Pune</a>, written for semi-arid conditions. NRCG says the schedule may vary with climate, soil and variety. Use the days as a guide and let the vine's stage decide.

365	Day 8 to 17: check bud break and count shoots
366	Sprouting should begin around day 8 to 10. By about day 15, walk through the block and pick ten vines from different rows. Count the sprouted shoots on each and note which ones carry a bunch. Mark any patch where sprouting is weak, so you can check it again at thinning.
367	NRCG's <a href="https://nrcgrapes.icar.gov.in/canopy-management.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">canopy management page</a> gives the targets for fruit pruning: <strong>0.75 shoots per square foot for export and 1 shoot per square foot for local market</strong>. At 10 x 6 feet spacing, one vine has 60 square feet. By our arithmetic that is about 45 shoots for export and 60 for local market. Work it out again for your own spacing.
368	Day 14 to 20: thin shoots early
369	NRCG advises thinning at the <strong>4 to 5 leaf stage</strong>, because this avoids loss of stored food. Its calendar says removing extra shoots right after the bunches appear gives air in the small canopy. That air also helps sprays reach the bunch before flowering.
370	Start with the second and third shoots from one bud. Then remove crowded shoots until you reach your number, and tie the rest at even distance on the wires. In <a href="https://core.ac.uk/download/357743294.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">one NRCG trial on Tas-A-Ganesh grapes</a>, thinning to about 15 shoots per metre, or about 40 per vine, gave higher yield than unthinned vines. That is one variety on one rootstock, and your vineyard may differ.
371	Day 25 to 30: how many bunches to keep
372	Thin bunches before flowering. NRCG's calendar gives these targets:
373	Export: 1 bunch per 1.5 square feet.
374	Local market: 0.75 bunch per square foot.
375	Raisin: 1.5 to 2 bunches per square foot.
376	Cane under 6 mm thick: one bunch, with one supporting shoot.
377	Cane 8 to 10 mm thick: two bunches on a shoot, plus one supporting shoot.
378	Keep 10 to 12 leaves above each export bunch. NRCG's canopy page says 8 to 10 is enough for local market.
379	What the bunch should look like
380	You want a clean stalk with no brown or pink patches, and flower buds that stay on when you shake the bunch gently. <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/1812/447" rel="noopener" target="_blank">A study from Pune, Nashik and Sangli vineyards</a>, pruned in the third or fourth week of October, describes the warning signs:
381	Flower parts drop when the bunch is shaken slightly.
382	The main stalk turns brown, reddish or pinkish.
383	Sunken brown spots appear on berries of 5 to 11 mm.
384	Tag any bunch with a pink or red patch on the stalk. In that study, such bunches looked fine at first, but the part beyond the patch collapsed a few weeks later.
385	Downy mildew: dew counts as much as rain
386	NRCG scientists report that <a href="https://core.ac.uk/download/357743414.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">rain and heavy dew help downy mildew start</a>, and that risk to the young bunch is very high in the first 55 to 60 days after pruning. So your whole 45 days sits inside the risk period.
387	That study found infection conditions under dew alone: 95 to 100% humidity at night and leaves wet for at least 4 to 12 hours. Bunches can stay wet longer than leaves because they are compact. A forecast of "no rain" does not mean a safe week.
388	Know the signs. <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/grapes_diseases_1.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a> describes yellowish, see-through spots on the upper leaf and white growth below. Shoots may be stunted with brown, sunken marks. In the Maharashtra study, though, bunches died with no white growth at all. Do not wait to see white powder on a bunch.
389	Check the bunches at dawn. If they are wet, note that night as a risk night. Read <a href="https://nrcgrapes.icar.gov.in/node/815" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NRCG's weekly grape advisory</a> for your district. One issue we read covered Nashik, Pune, Solapur and Sangli in Maharashtra, and Belagavi, Bagalkot, Vijayapura, Bidar and Bengaluru Rural in Karnataka. The bunch study was done in Maharashtra, so Karnataka growers should go by their own district forecast.
390	Plan protection before a wet spell, not after you see damage. For spray choice and dose, follow the current recommendation of NRCG, your state agricultural university or KVK.

439	Mango before flowering: what to do in the next 30 days
440	November to early December mango care in India: when to cut water and nitrogen, how to read a late flush, and what to watch and not spray at bloom.
441	<a href="/">Home</a> / <a href="/articles">Articles</a> / Mango before flowering: what to do in the next...
442	Mango before flowering: what to do in the next 30 days
443	November to early December mango care in India: when to cut water and nitrogen, how to read a late flush, and what to watch and not spray at bloom.
444	In November and early December, stop frequent irrigation and give no extra nitrogen to bearing mango trees. Let shoots mature and avoid heavy pruning. Note the date of any late flush. Scout for hoppers and white powder as panicles emerge, finish needed sprays before full bloom, and do not spray insecticide or fungicide in bloom.
445	See also: <a href="/crops/mango">Mango guide</a> and <a href="/products/immunity-mango-special">Immunity Mango Special</a>.
446	Each shoot tip on a mango tree will soon become either a flower panicle or more leaves. You cannot change the weather. You can avoid the mistakes that push the tree toward leaves, and you can be ready when panicles appear. This page covers the next 30 days only. For the full year, see the <a href="/crops/mango">mango guide</a>. If your tree skips whole years, read about <a href="/crop-problems/mango-alternate-bearing">alternate bearing</a>.
447	First, find your own flowering date
448	Everything below counts back from the date your trees usually flower. That date changes with region, variety and year. In the Konkan, <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/411/1411" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Alphonso flowering normally starts in November</a>. <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/2928/1816" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Junagadh research</a> says Kesar flowering is sensitive to climate and is often delayed by flushes after the monsoon. In Saurashtra, <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/61513/25092/154941" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a rise in temperature brought unusual Kesar flowering in February and March 2011</a>.
449	So use your own records from the last three years, not your neighbour's date. Junagadh Agricultural University's <a href="https://www.jau.in/storage/document/dynamic_document//fruit_science_recommendations_20260620_1720.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">climate note for Kesar</a> gives 25 to 30°C days and 15 to 18°C nights as ideal during flowering. A warm or wet November can move your date.
450	Water: reduce it, and know why
451	Published advice differs, so read it as a range:
452	The <a href="https://www.nhb.gov.in/pdf/fruits/mango/man006.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">National Horticulture Board</a> says frequent irrigation should be avoided 2 to 3 months before the flowering season.
453	<a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_fruits_mango.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU, for Tamil Nadu</a>, says to avoid irrigation and fertigation for 30 days before flowering, and to start again as soon as flowering begins.
454	A <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/pdf/Management%20of%20Production%20Problems%20in%20Tropical%20Fruit%20Crops.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU chapter on mango</a> warns that irrigation during flower bud formation can turn a flower bud into a leaf bud.
455	The reason matters. A <a href="https://www.mango.org/es/mango-flowering-review_italo-cavalcante-atual-2" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2022 research review</a> says dry soil does not itself make flowers. It stops new shoots from coming, so the existing leaves can grow older. The Junagadh recommendation lists we read give no stop date for Gujarat, so ask your KVK for your soil.
456	What to do now:
457	If panicles usually come in January, stop frequent irrigation now.
458	On drip, stop fertigation as well.
459	Do not give one heavy watering "to help" during this period.
460	This is for bearing trees. A <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/pdf/tech_bulletin/national/IPM-Mango-Revised-Sept2011.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">national pest schedule</a>, written from research in north India, lists thatch cover and irrigation for young plants in December and January.
461	Nitrogen: close this door
462	The same review reports that high nitrogen during flower induction can harm panicle emergence or cause mixed shoots. TNAU's Tamil Nadu timing for manures and fertilisers is <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_fruits_mango.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">September to October</a>. Junagadh's schedule for rejuvenated Kesar orchards in South Saurashtra puts the <a href="https://www.jau.in/storage/document/dynamic_document//fruit_science_recommendations_20260620_1720.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">second dose in February</a>.
463	So do not apply urea or any nitrogen-rich dose in this window, by soil or by drip. If you missed your autumn dose, do not try to make it up now.
464	Shoot maturity: count the days
465	Mango flowers at the tip of <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/cm-mango-eng.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">mature wood</a>. Sources differ on how old that wood must be. TNAU's canopy bulletin says six weeks or older. A <a href="https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/download/411/1411" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Konkan paper on Alphonso</a> says a new flush after the monsoon needs another 80 to 100 days. Use both as a rough guide:

Tag ten shoots on a few trees and write down the date each flush began. A date in your notebook is better than a guess.
482	If a late flush has started
483	A late flush means those shoots may not be ready when the cold comes. The Konkan paper reports that many shoots produced after the monsoon do not flower. It links them to late rain, cloud, heat and humidity in September and October. New leaves also bring pests: <a href="https://aems.nau.in/Main/view_experiment/1443" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Navsari Agricultural University</a> notes hopper intensity is severe during vegetative flush and flowering.
484	Keep water and nitrogen off these trees.
485	Do not prune hard. TNAU says the heavier the pruning, the more vigorous and numerous the flushes.
486	Tip pruning is the one option to discuss. TNAU says young flushes cut back to mature wood may give a floral flush. In the Konkan trial, tip pruning done in the last fortnight of November brought earlier panicles in Alphonso. That is one variety in one place, so try a few branches first.
487	Do not start a growth regulator now. Junagadh's paclobutrazol advice for rejuvenated Kesar orchards is a soil application in mid-July.
488	What to watch when panicles emerge
489	<strong>Hoppers.</strong> A <a href="https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/edd5ace3-3519-4279-b457-5bcba2f393aa/content" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Navsari thesis from Paria, Valsad</a> found higher hopper numbers at flower initiation and full bloom, and flowering induced a month early brought hoppers earlier too. The national schedule asks for scouting once a fortnight, one yellow sticky trap per five trees, and pruning crowded branches in December. It places the first spray at early panicle stage if hoppers exceed 5 to 10 per panicle, and the next at full panicle length. <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/aas/mango_ex.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a> uses 10 per panicle and says the second spray must come before full bloom. For the product and dose, follow the current recommendation of your state agricultural university or KVK.
490	<strong>Powdery mildew.</strong> Look for <a href="https://www.nhb.gov.in/pdf/fruits/mango/man002.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">white powdery growth</a> on panicle stalks, flowers and young fruit. Affected flowers may not open and can fall. Do not judge by weather alone. Several Indian sources link the disease to mist, rain or high humidity with cool nights. In <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/52502/1/Agrometeorology%202020%20PM%20on%20mango.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a Valsad study</a>, symptoms began as panicles started, and severity was linked to low humidity. Check panicles from the day they appear, and take local spray advice at the start of panicle growth.
491	<strong>Mealybug.</strong> Where this is a known problem, the national schedule lists raking soil around the trunk and fastening a 25 cm plastic band on the trunk in November and December, 30 cm above the ground, to stop young nymphs from climbing.
492	What not to spray in bloom
493	The national schedule is direct: insecticides and fungicides should be avoided during flower opening and pollination. So finish protection before full bloom. If hoppers rise during full bloom, call your KVK before you spray. Keep <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/pdf/tech_bulletin/national/IPM-Mango-Revised-Sept2011.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">bee hives in the orchard</a> if you can. For any other spray in bloom, read the label and ask why you need it. After rain, check again: TNAU notes rain in flowering <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/pdf/Management%20of%20Production%20Problems%20in%20Tropical%20Fruit%20Crops.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">washes away pollen and encourages hoppers</a>. What happens after fruit set is covered in <a href="/crop-problems/mango-fruit-drop">mango fruit drop</a>.

522	Mild vs harsh seaweed extraction: what reaches your plant
523	Extraction decides what stays whole, what breaks and what new compounds form. Reviews report that alkali and heat cut long seaweed sugars and can degrade some hormone-like molecules, while water-based extracts are reportedly rich in hormone-like activity. Harsher is not always weaker in trials, so ask each seller for species, route, pH and batch report.
524	Two bottles can both say "seaweed extract" and still be quite different liquids. The seaweed may even be the same. What differs is what was done to it between the shore and the bottle. That step is rarely printed on the pack, so here is what researchers have written about it, and what you can ask.
525	What extraction has to do
526	Seaweed does not give up its contents easily. Alginate, a main sugar of brown seaweed, sits in the plant as <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/1660-3397/23/9/366/xml" rel="noopener" target="_blank">water-insoluble salts</a>, and a 2025 review links its extraction to alkaline conditions. So makers use chemicals, heat, pressure or machines to get the material out.
527	This is a trade-off. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">One review</a> says the method must handle the complex make-up of seaweed and also protect the integrity of the active molecules. Stronger conditions can pull out more. They can also change what comes out.
528	The main routes in plain words
529	Route
530	What is done
531	What the literature reports
532	Alkaline
533	Seaweed is treated with caustic potash or caustic soda, at 70 to 100°C in one description. The most widely used version runs at high pressure
534	High extraction. Long sugars are cut into smaller pieces. New compounds form that were not in the seaweed. Some hormone-like molecules can be degraded
535	Acid
536	Fresh chopped seaweed is held with sulphuric or hydrochloric acid, at 40 to 50°C for 30 minutes in one description
537	Removes complex phenolic compounds and cuts sugars into smaller pieces. Mainly used for fucose-rich sugars
538	Water
539	Dried seaweed is blended and soaked in water, then filtered
540	Reportedly rich in hormone-like activity. One review says the efficiency of water-based methods for seaweed gums is considered too low
541	Enzyme
542	Enzymes break down molecules in the cell walls
543	Turns parts that do not dissolve into parts that do. In 2019, one review found no published work on this route with Ascophyllum
544	Cold or mechanical
545	Seaweed is crushed or milled, then pressed or filtered, without a chemical cooking step
546	One 2022 paper says this keeps the actives and holds pH near 4.5
547	Sources for the table: <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Ascophyllum review</a>, <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">seaweed extract review</a>, <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/1660-3397/23/9/366/xml" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2025 extraction review</a>, <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.1017925/pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2022 study of commercial products</a>.
548	Gujarat has its own example of the mechanical route. CSIR-CSMCRI, a national research institute in Gujarat, developed a process in which <a href="https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/technologies/integrated-processes-simultaneous-production-sap-and-k-carrageenan-fresh-seaweed-0" rel="noopener" target="_blank">fresh seaweed is ground and pressed near the harvest site</a>. The sap is obtained without heat-and-chemical cycles and without added water. The same page notes that preservatives can be added before sale.
549	Which compounds are reported to be sensitive
550	<strong>Long sugars.</strong> Alkali and acid both cut them into shorter chains. One review calls this <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">moderate degradation</a> in the alkaline route.
551	<strong>Hormone-like molecules.</strong> The same review says small molecules such as hormones may be significantly degraded during extraction. A <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review of Ascophyllum products</a> says the wide variation in reported auxin is likely linked to extraction and processing, along with where and when the seaweed was harvested.
552	<strong>Phenolic compounds.</strong> Acid removes some. Alkali <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">turns them into many different reaction products</a>.
553	<strong>Heat-sensitive substances in general.</strong> A <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/1660-3397/23/9/366/xml" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2025 review</a> warns that too much temperature and processing time can decompose valuable substances. It also cites one seaweed in which higher drying temperatures and longer processing reduced chlorophyll and phenolic compounds.
554	Harsher does not always mean weaker
555	It would be easy to stop here and say mild is good and harsh is bad. The studies do not allow that.
556	The review that warns about hormone loss also says alkaline extraction at high pressure <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000310" rel="noopener" target="_blank">has been found effective</a>. In <a href="https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df561a63b8185d9cac6189" rel="noopener" target="_blank">one study of a brown seaweed, Macrocystis</a>, extracts were made at pH 8 to 12 and at 40 to 80°C. All of them promoted tomato growth and rooting in mung bean cuttings, and the highest auxin-type activity came from the extract made at pH 11 and 80°C. That is one seaweed in one study, so it is not a rule.
557	Other work shows that the method changes how an extract behaves, without showing which is better. <a href="https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/comparative-transcriptome-analysis-of-two-ascophyllum-nodosum-ext/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Two commercial Ascophyllum extracts</a> tested on Arabidopsis, a small laboratory plant, both increased plant weight. But one changed about 4.5% of the genes measured and the other less than 1%, and their polyphenol levels differed about four times. The <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548832" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Ascophyllum review</a> also says alkaline and acid extracts acted through different routes in plants under drought, in work on soybean and Arabidopsis.
558	So what reaches your plant does depend on extraction. Which route suits your crop is not settled.
559	At our own plant at GIDC Shihor, Bhavnagar, <a href="/factory-and-rd">we extract seaweed in-house</a> by a mild method, without harsh chemicals, very high pH or heat. That is the route we chose. The studies above did not test our process, and we do not claim they prove it will do better on your farm.
560	What you can and cannot tell from the bottle
561	Do not expect the method on the label. In a <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.1017925/pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">2022 study of 20 commercial products</a>, the authors found that labelling was not uniform and a detailed composition was not usual, though seaweed percentage usually was given. Here is what that study suggests about the clues you do have.
562	<strong>Colour and smell.</strong> It describes chemically extracted products as dark, and cold-extracted ones as green to light brown with a marine smell. This is a general description, not a test.
563	<strong>pH.</strong> The products ranged from below 4 to above 10, mainly because of extraction method and seaweed percentage. But pH alone cannot show the method. In <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5897654" rel="noopener" target="_blank">another study</a>, extracts made with acid were afterwards brought near neutral with potassium hydroxide.
564	<strong>Potassium.</strong> Alkaline processing can give high potassium, but the same paper says potassium may also come from the seaweed tissue. A high figure proves nothing by itself.
565	We did not find a reliable published test that shows the extraction method at home, so we are not giving you one.
566	What to ask the seller
567	Which seaweed species, by its full name?
568	Which route: alkali, acid, water, enzyme or mechanical? Was the seaweed fresh or dried first?
569	Roughly what temperature and pH? A maker may keep the recipe private but should be able to name the route.
570	What are the pH and potassium of this batch, and can I see the report? Our <a href="/quality">quality page</a> shows the kind of checks to ask any supplier about.
571	Is any preservative added?
572	How do you keep one batch like the next? The authors of the two-extract study call standardising these products a major challenge.
573	Do not buy only because a pack says "mild" or "cold". That includes ours. Ask what the words mean.
574	Do not reject a dark product on colour alone.
575	Do not change the dose because a product is called mild or strong. Follow the dose on the pack.
576	Do not expect any study result on your own field. Results vary with crop, soil, region and season. Try a new product on a small strip beside an unsprayed strip before you use it on the whole field.

598	Potato bulking stage: what to do in these 30 days
599	In the bulking month, keep soil evenly moist with light, frequent irrigation. Keep every tuber covered with soil. Protect leaves before cool, cloudy, humid weather brings late blight. Stop irrigation about 7 to 15 days before haulm cutting, longer on heavy soil. Avoid long dry gaps followed by heavy flooding.
600	Tubers now grow in size and weight. Our <a href="/crops/potato">potato guide</a> puts this stage at about 8 weeks to harvest, and it varies by variety. A crop planted in mid October reaches it around December. The <a href="/knowledge/potato-fertilizer-schedule">fertilizer schedule</a> covers what to apply, and <a href="/crop-problems/potato-small-tubers">why potatoes stay small</a> covers diagnosis. This page covers only the field work for the next 30 days.
601	Day 1: walk the field and fix your dates
602	Dig a few plants from different parts of the field. See how many tubers are set and whether any are near the surface.
603	Squeeze soil from tuber depth (see the test below).
604	Write down your planned haulm cutting date. Your last irrigation is counted back from this date.
605	Days 1 to 20: keep the water even
606	ICAR-CPRI says the crop needs <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151957/54627/414083" rel="noopener" target="_blank">light and frequent irrigation</a>, mainly on light soils. It also advises keeping available soil moisture at 65 to 85 percent during active growth. <a href="https://www.fao.org/land-water/databases-and-software/crop-information/potato/fr" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAO</a> reports that using more than 50 percent of the available soil water lowers yield, and that shortage early in yield formation increases spindle-shaped tubers.

614	Alternate days at 125 percent of CPE; gaps over 3 days may cause stress
615	ICAR-CPRI review, India
616	Sprinkler
617	Twice a week at 150 percent of CPE
618	ICAR-CPRI review, India
619	Micro-sprinkler
620	<a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/download/131327/54389/410681" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Every 4 days</a>, 60 minutes per run at this stage
621	ICAR-CIAH Bikaner, sandy arid soil, that system only
622	CPE is cumulative pan evaporation. Your nearest KVK or weather station can give the figure.
623	North Gujarat soils can be light. The trial soil at the <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/PotatoJ/article/download/144730/54285/522206" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SDAU station at Aseda</a> was loamy sand. In one two-season trial at <a href="https://arccjournals.com/journal/agricultural-science-digest/ARCC941" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SDAU Sardarkrushinagar</a> on a processing variety, drip with a lateral in each row gave more processing-grade tubers than perforated pipe, micro-sprinkler and furrow. In the Bikaner study, micro-sprinklers out-yielded drip. So results depend on place and system. Do not change your system mid-season. Run the one you have evenly.
624	Check moisture by hand. <a href="https://extension.umn.edu/irrigation/estimating-soil-moisture-feel-and-appearance-method" rel="noopener" target="_blank">University of Minnesota</a> gives these signs:
625	<strong>Loamy sand, enough water:</strong> looks moist, makes a weak ball or slightly sticks together.
626	<strong>Loamy sand, too dry:</strong> will not form a ball under pressure.
627	<strong>Sandy loam, enough water:</strong> fairly dark, makes a good ball.
628	<strong>Sandy loam, too dry:</strong> only lightly coloured, will not ball.
629	<strong>Either soil, very wet:</strong> leaves a wet outline on your hand.
630	Too much water also harms. CPRI says excess moisture creates aeration problems and favours some diseases. Too much water combined with late heavy rain can raise post-harvest rot. <a href="https://www.ag.ndsu.edu/PUBLICATIONS/crops/potato-tuber-second-growth/a1987.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NDSU</a> links moisture stress to second growth and advises uniform moisture without overwatering. <a href="https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-potatoes" rel="noopener" target="_blank">University of Minnesota</a> links swings between too much and too little water to hollow heart. FAO also notes that frequent irrigation with cold water can cool soil below the 15 to 18°C it gives as best for tuber formation.
631	Earthing: repair, do not rebuild
632	CPRI's guidance for the plains is that <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151953/54624/414076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">hoeing, done with earthing up, should not go beyond 30 days</a> after planting (45 days in the hills). So the main earthing should already be done. Practice varies: <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_vegetables_potato.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TNAU</a>, writing for Tamil Nadu, advises earthing at 60 days to keep tuber moth off exposed tubers.
633	Now walk the ridges, especially after each irrigation. Where a ridge has cracked or washed, pull soil over the tuber by hand. Do not run a machine through a closed crop. <a href="https://u.osu.edu/vegnetnews/?p=2704" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Ohio State University</a> warns that hilling after vines flop and begin to close rows can be damaging.
634	CPRI says <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151959/54629/414087" rel="noopener" target="_blank">light turns tubers green</a> and builds up toxic glycoalkaloids. <a href="https://ndsu.edu/agriculture/node/5220" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NDSU</a> says never to eat tubers green beneath the skin, and to discard those with extensive greening.
635	All 30 days: blight and frost watch
636	CPRI says late blight spreads at <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151964/54634/414099" rel="noopener" target="_blank">15 to 20°C with humidity above 80 percent</a>. Its <a href="https://cpri.icar.gov.in/AddLink/getIMG/6065" rel="noopener" target="_blank">disease bulletin</a> adds that cloudy weather favours it. That bulletin records first appearance in 2015-16 on 27 November at Modipuram, 8 December at Pantnagar, 12 December at Ludhiana and 30 January at Kalyani in West Bengal. Dates shift each year. We found no published date for Banaskantha, so watch the same weather signs and ask your KVK or the Potato and Sorghum Research Station at <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/748809" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Deesa</a>.
637	Look for water-soaked, pale green patches near leaf tips and edges, and brown marks circling stems. On tubers, look for reddish-brown to purple patches. CPRI says tubers get infected when rain or dew carries spores down from diseased leaves.
638	What to do:
639	Do not wait for spots. CPRI advises a protective spray before the canopy closes, reaching the lower leaves too.
640	If blight appears, CPRI advises changing to a different fungicide type. Follow the current recommendation of your state agricultural university or KVK for product and dose.
641	CPRI's bulletin says to stop irrigation when weather turns favourable for blight. Keep this delay short, and restart when the weather clears.
642	Frost is the other risk in the north. A <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/PotatoJ/article/download/129404/49484/543559" rel="noopener" target="_blank">review on Punjab</a> reports that below 1°C for more than 4 to 5 hours can cause 50 percent foliage loss in a night, and that damage is greater at 50 to 60 days after planting than at 80 to 90 days. The Bikaner study reports that micro-sprinklers protected the crop from low temperatures.
643	Last stretch: stop water, then cut haulms
644	Count back from haulm cutting, not from digging.
645	CPRI's post-harvest guidance: stop irrigation at least one week before haulm cutting, so the skin cures and digging is easier.
646	CPRI's seed crop guidance: about 10 days before in light soil and 15 days in heavy soil.
647	Seed crop haulm killing dates from the same guidance: end of December for Punjab, Haryana and western UP; by 10 January for central UP and MP; by 15 January for eastern UP, Bihar, West Bengal and Odisha.
648	Those dates are for seed crops. A crop grown for eating may follow a different date. For Gujarat, confirm your date locally. CPRI advises harvesting <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151953/54624/414076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">15 to 20 days after haulm killing</a>, when the field is workable and the skin has hardened. Rub a tuber with your thumb: if the skin peels, it is not ready.
649	If blight has badly taken over, CPRI's bulletin says to cut haulms at 75 percent disease severity.
650	What to avoid
651	A long dry gap followed by a heavy flood.
652	Irrigating into cool, cloudy, humid weather.
653	Late urea. CPRI notes <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151953/54624/414076" rel="noopener" target="_blank">excess nitrogen</a> increases undesirable extra-large tubers.
654	Leaving the crop in the ground too long after haulm killing. CPRI reports that <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/151964/54634/414099" rel="noopener" target="_blank">late-harvested crops show more black scurf</a>.
655	Digging wet soil, which CPRI links to soft rot.

676	Which matters more if water is short: early growth or tuber growth?
677	FAO reports that shortage during stolon formation, tuber initiation and yield formation costs the most. It says water can be restricted more safely in the early vegetative and ripening periods. If supply is limited, FAO advises directing it to get the best yield per hectare, rather than spreading it thinly.
678	How much water does a potato crop need in total?
679	FAO gives 500 to 700 mm for high yields from a 120 to 150 day crop. An ICAR-CPRI review gives about 300 to 600 mm under conventional systems in India. Your need varies with soil, weather and crop length.
680	Can soil moisture affect common scab?
681	ICAR-CPRI says that keeping soil moisture high for 10 to 20 days after tuber initiation can help reduce common scab. It does not remove the need for clean seed.
682	Is drip useful if my irrigation water is salty?
683	An ICAR-CPRI review says drip is advantageous where water is saline, because water goes below the canopy. FAO rates potato as moderately sensitive to soil salinity. Get your water tested first.

687	What do EC, pH, SAR and RSC on your borewell report mean for rabi?
688	What EC, pH, SAR and RSC mean on a borewell water report, safe limits from FAO and IS 11624, rabi crop sensitivity, and what to do before sowing.
689	Read EC first for salt: FAO calls below 0.7 dS/m free of restriction and above 3 severe. Read SAR together with EC. RSC above 2.5 me/L marks alkali water under IS 11624. Onion and gram are sensitive, wheat is more tolerant. Leach before sowing, use gypsum only for high RSC.
690	See also: <a href="/knowledge/soil-health">Soil health</a> and <a href="/products">the Immunity range</a>.
691	First check the units
692	FAO says <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E01.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">dS/m and mmho/cm are the same thing</a>. If your report shows µS/cm, divide by 1,000 to get dS/m. RSC in me/L is the same as meq/L.
693	What each number tells you
694	<strong>EC</strong> is the total salt in the water. High salt makes it harder for roots to take up water.
695	<strong>pH</strong> shows if the water is acid or alkaline. It does not tell you how much salt or sodium is present.
696	<strong>SAR</strong> compares sodium with calcium and magnesium. High sodium can break soil structure, so water enters the soil slowly.
697	<strong>RSC</strong> is <a href="https://cssri.res.in/PSSU/Publications/AICRP/11.%20File%205-BIS%20Guidelines%2011624%20%202019.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">carbonate plus bicarbonate, minus calcium plus magnesium</a>. A high RSC marks alkali water. Over years, <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/25364/1/GW%20use%20soil%20sodicity%20AWM%20-Minhhas%20et%20al.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">alkali groundwater can make soil sodic</a>.

729	FAO says a restriction <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E01.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">does not mean the water is unsuitable</a>. It means you must manage more carefully. FAO also assumes good drainage, no water table within 2 metres, and sandy-loam to clay-loam soil. Its limits are too strict for drip.

768	The EC figures come from <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAO Table 4</a> and assume 15 to 20 percent of the water drains below the roots. 769	For sodium, wheat is far more tolerant than gram or onion. <a href="https://cssri.res.in/PSSU/Publications/AICRP/11.%20File%205-BIS%20Guidelines%2011624%20%202019.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IS 11624 Table 8</a> puts wheat at ESP 30 to 50, chickpea at 16 to 20 and onion at 20 to 25. ESP means exchangeable sodium percentage, a soil test.
770	Evidence on cumin is thin. In a <a href="https://core.ac.uk/download/236023127.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">pot study at CAZRI, Jodhpur</a>, water of EC 10 cut seed yield in all five genotypes, by about 24% in the best and over 50% in the worst. In a <a href="https://core.ac.uk/download/525310314.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">study in Iran</a>, the authors called EC 5 the upper limit for an economic crop. Varieties differ, so ask your KVK or state agricultural university.
771	In Gujarat, the main problem may be salt, not RSC. A <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JISCAR/article/view/136568" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Junagadh Agricultural University survey</a> of 180 wells in Gir Somnath found EC from 1.15 to 12.39 dS/m, yet 92.2% of samples were safe for RSC. That is one district, so your well may differ.
772	What you can do this season
773	<strong>Leach before sowing.</strong> FAO says a <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">pre-plant leaching irrigation</a> is often used to remove salt from the top soil, and that the cool season is better for leaching. This works only if the field drains.
774	<strong>Use your best water first.</strong> If you have canal or pond water, <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/25364/1/GW%20use%20soil%20sodicity%20AWM%20-Minhhas%20et%20al.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">start with it</a> for pre-sowing and early growth.
775	<strong>Change sources rather than mix, if you can.</strong> One review reports that <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/25364/1/GW%20use%20soil%20sodicity%20AWM%20-Minhhas%20et%20al.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">crops under alternating use tended to do better</a> than crops on mixed water. Blending can still <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">dilute a high-SAR water</a>.
776	<strong>Watch for crusting.</strong> FAO warns that <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E04.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">low-salt water after very salty, high-SAR water</a> can make water entry much worse.
777	<strong>Irrigate more often where you can.</strong> This <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">reduces water stress</a> between irrigations.
778	<strong>Do not sow in the centre of a raised bed.</strong> <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Salt collects there</a>. FAO suggests two rows, away from the centre.
779	<strong>Take care with fertiliser.</strong> Fertilisers are <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">mostly soluble salts</a>, so placement and timing matter.
780	Gypsum is for high RSC and sodium. It does not take salt out of water. FAO says it is <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E04.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">especially effective when water EC is below 0.5</a> and much less effective in very salty water.
781	FAO's figure is that <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E04.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">1 me/L of calcium needs 86 kg of pure gypsum per 1,000 cubic metres of water</a>. That is 10 cm of water on one hectare. As an example, to bring RSC 6 down to 2.5, the sum is 3.5 x 86, or about 301 kg of pure gypsum. At 70% purity it is about 430 kg. Ask your lab or KVK to confirm your target.
782	Gypsum can go <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/25364/1/GW%20use%20soil%20sodicity%20AWM%20-Minhhas%20et%20al.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">in a bed, or as clods in the water channel</a>. A <a href="https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/banking/pdf/Land%20Development_Reclaiming%20Sodic%20Water%20for%20Irrigation%20through%20Gypsum%20Chamber.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">model chamber design on TNAU Agritech</a> uses 25 to 50 mm lumps and is refilled after 40 to 50 hours of running. One review says wheat after rice usually <a href="https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/25364/1/GW%20use%20soil%20sodicity%20AWM%20-Minhhas%20et%20al.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">responds when RSC is above 5</a>, but on light soils with fallow kharif it may not respond below 10. Do not add sulphur to irrigation water: FAO calls it <a href="https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E04.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">not satisfactory for water application</a>. Gypsum supply and subsidy differ by state and change over time, so check with your state agriculture department.

Rinse a clean bottle with the same water, fill it, and write your name, village, well depth, crop and soil type. See also our <a href="/knowledge/soil-health">soil health page</a>.

800	Do I need a soil test as well as a water test?
801	Yes. The water report shows what you are adding to the field. A soil test shows what has already built up. FAO estimates root zone salt from water salt only by assuming 15 to 20 percent of the water drains below the roots, and many fields do not match that.
802	My report gives TDS in mg/L and no EC. How do I read it?
803	FAO pairs TDS with EC in the same table. Below 450 mg/L matches the no-restriction class, 450 to 2,000 mg/L the slight to moderate class, and above 2,000 mg/L the severe class.
804	Can very pure water also cause problems?
805	Yes. FAO says water with EC below 0.2 dS/m almost always causes water entry problems, because it washes calcium and salts from the surface soil. The soil then disperses and seals.
806	Can water above EC 4 be used in kharif?
807	IS 11624 says water of EC 4 dS/m or more should be kept for winter-season crops. In an emergency it allows no more than one or two protective irrigations to kharif crops.
808	What about boron on the report?
809	IS 11624 rates boron below 1.0 mg/L as low, and above 4.0 mg/L as very high. Its crop table lists wheat and potato as semi-tolerant (1.0 to 2.0 mg/L) and onion as tolerant (2.0 to 4.0 mg/L).

812	At which crop stage should you spray seaweed extract?
813	Trials have tested seaweed sprays at seedling, vegetative, early flowering and full bloom stages, and before heat or dry spells. The most field evidence is for early flowering, and few trials compare stages. Avoid the hottest hours. Stronger sprays often did no better and sometimes did worse, so follow the dose on the pack.
814	Most seaweed advice is one line: spray at flowering. The research is more useful than that, and more careful. It shows which stages were tested, how often, and where a stronger spray did no better. It also shows how much has not been tested.

843	These are results from particular extracts and places. A rating of medium means the work is reasonably good, not that it will repeat on your farm. Results vary by region, soil and variety. None of the trials we read was on mango, so for the current <a href="/crops/mango">mango</a> pre-flowering period there is no direct evidence here to lean on.
844	Seedling and early growth
845	In <a href="https://pub.isa-india.in/index.php/ija/article/download/4374/4049/4569" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a maize trial in the North Eastern Himalayas</a>, seed soaked for 24 hours in 5% Kappaphycus sap gave 94% germination, against 83% for seed soaked in water. Papaya seedlings in <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/2223-7747/14/3/317" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a Brazilian greenhouse study</a> were sprayed weekly from 12 or 20 days after sowing. At this stage, the strength of the spray matters a great deal, as shown further below.
846	In the same maize trial, sprays were given at 25, 50 and 75 days after sowing. At <a href="https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2023.1273355/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CSMCRI, Bhavnagar</a>, pot-grown maize was sprayed at the 5th, 10th and 15th leaf stages in one rabi season. In <a href="https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JWR/article/view/152738" rel="noopener" target="_blank">wheat in the Terai region of West Bengal</a>, over two rabi seasons, seed treatment plus two sprays at tillering and heading gave significantly more spikes per square metre. So in these three trials the first spray came once the crop was established, and later sprays followed at the next growth stages.
847	Before flowering and at fruit set
848	In <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.983772" rel="noopener" target="_blank">tomato in Italy</a>, three sprays were given at first visible flower cluster, first open flower, and when five or more clusters were in flower. Fruit yield rose in the climate chamber, greenhouse and open field. Gene changes were broader after the second and third sprays than after the first, which the authors read as a cumulative effect.
849	In <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1655340/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">pepper and eggplant in Bulgaria</a>, over two years in the open field, two sprays at early flowering and full bloom gave higher yield, mainly through more fruits per plant. The study did not compare other stages or doses. So it does not show that flowering is better than any other time.
850	The result was less clear in <a href="https://scielo.br/j/rbf/a/6FHYMmqqkmKXMvRcrqj97FN" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Gala apple in Brazil</a>, with one spray at full bloom. In the first season fruit set was about 7% above the unsprayed trees. In the second season the seaweed spray had no effect on fruit set. If your flowers are dropping, first check the basics, including <a href="/micronutrients/boron/boron-flowering-fruit-set">boron at flowering</a>.
851	Before heat or a dry spell
852	The clearest timing study is <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.00807/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a tomato trial in a growth chamber</a>. Dwarf tomato plants with young flower buds were sprayed 3 days before a 14-day heat period, at 31 degrees by day and 24 at night, and again after it. Heat cut viable pollen by 80% in unsprayed plants, and both extracts raised it. Only one extract, made by hot alkaline processing, significantly raised fruit number. The extract made by a gentle low-temperature process did not. So the process can change the result, and not always in the way a seller says. Without heat, neither extract gave a significant gain in fruit number.
853	Drought work points the same way. In <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5751077" rel="noopener" target="_blank">soybean in a growth chamber</a>, the extract was given with the feed twice a week from planting, then water was stopped. No gene differences were seen before the stress, only once it began. In <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2026.1859338/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">greenhouse tomato in Bulgaria</a>, three sprays during flowering, 10 to 12 days apart, had limited effect under full irrigation, while some treatments gave higher yield under reduced irrigation. <a href="https://doaj.org/article/712b89b388a848219c56be85680b3b1a" rel="noopener" target="_blank">A dwarf tomato study</a> with flowering-time sprays also reported better drought tolerance.
854	The practical reading: spray before the expected stress, and do not expect much in an easy season.
855	How often, and at what time of day
856	The gaps between sprays in these trials ran from weekly in papaya seedlings to 25 days in maize. We did not find a trial that compared spray intervals directly, so no interval is proven. In the maize trial, net returns rose significantly only up to two sprays, at 25 and 50 days. A third spray did not significantly raise net returns further.
857	On time of day the evidence is thin. None of the papers we read compared morning with evening. The Bulgarian trial says only that sprays were <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1655340/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">kept away from the hottest time of day</a>, to prevent sunburn. That is the one clear rule we can support: avoid the hottest hours.
858	Why a stronger spray can do worse
859	Across the studies we read, this pattern came up again and again, though not in every measure.
860	<strong>Maize seed.</strong> In the <a href="https://pub.isa-india.in/index.php/ija/article/download/4374/4049/4569" rel="noopener" target="_blank">North Eastern Himalayas trial</a>, 15% sap cut germination to 75%, below the 83% of the water soak. Root length fell below the water soak from 10% sap.
861	<strong>Maize in pots.</strong> At <a href="https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2023.1273355/full" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Bhavnagar</a>, the best response was generally at the lower of two doses, and the highest dose of Sargassum was on par with no spray.
862	<strong>Tomato.</strong> In <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.983772" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Italy</a>, doubling the rate gave no more yield in the open field: 35% and 36% above control.
863	<strong>Apple.</strong> In <a href="https://scielo.br/j/rbf/a/6FHYMmqqkmKXMvRcrqj97FN" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Brazil</a>, 0.3% gave significantly more yield than 0.6% in the first season.
864	<strong>Papaya seedlings.</strong> In <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/2223-7747/14/3/317" rel="noopener" target="_blank">one greenhouse study</a>, several growth measures peaked at a middle dose, and with one extract root mass fell as dose rose under moderate temperatures. The best dose also changed with temperature.
865	<strong>Snapdragon in pots.</strong> A <a href="https://www.mdpi.com/2223-7747/13/16/2188/html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">glasshouse study</a> with extract mixed into potting soil found the best rate at the low end of the range, with harmful effects outside it.
866	These studies measured the effect. They did not settle the cause. The figures above belong to those saps and extracts. Sap strength and product strength are not the same thing, so do not copy them. A dose only makes sense for one product at its own strength. For the seaweed products from our own plant at <a href="/factory-and-rd">GIDC Shihor, Bhavnagar</a>, and for any other product, follow the dose on the pack.
867	What to do on your farm
868	Decide the job first: early growth, flowering, or a coming stress. Spray for that stage, not by habit.
869	If hot weather or a dry spell is forecast at flowering, plan the spray a few days ahead, as in the chamber trial.
870	Keep to the hours outside the hottest part of the day.
871	Leave one strip unsprayed, with the same fertiliser and water, and compare it at harvest.
872	Write down the date, stage and weather for each spray.
873	Do not raise the dose above the pack in the hope of more.
874	Do not use a strong solution on seed or young seedlings.
875	Do not add a third or fourth spray without checking that the earlier ones paid.
876	Do not cut fertiliser or irrigation because you are spraying seaweed.

889	Should I spray seaweed extract in a good season with no stress?
890	You can, but expect less. In the heat and irrigation studies described above, effects without stress were small or not significant. If money is short, keep the spray for a period when heat or short water is likely.
891	Does a result on dwarf tomato in a chamber apply to my field?
892	Not directly. Chamber studies control heat, water and light, and the dwarf tomato is a small plant used in laboratory studies. They help explain timing. Only field trials and your own unsprayed strip can tell you about your crop.
893	How many seasons do I need to judge a seaweed spray?
894	At least two. The apple study showed a small gain in fruit set in one season and none in the next, on the same trees. One good season can mislead you.
895	Can I use a concentration from a research paper?
896	No. Fresh sap and a finished product differ in strength, so the same percentage does not mean the same amount of seaweed. Follow the dose printed on the pack of the product you bought.
