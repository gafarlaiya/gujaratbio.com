543	Biofertilizers such as Azotobacter and PSB can go in with the manure.

547	Irrigate lightly after earthing up.

870	The law of the minimum
871	A crop grows only as well as its scarcest nutrient allows. Think of a bucket made of wooden staves of different heights: water fills only up to the shortest stave. If zinc or boron is the short stave, more urea or DAP raises the other staves but not the water level. This is why yields can stay flat while fertilizer bills rise.
872	Hidden hunger
873	Yield is often lost before any symptom appears. A crop slightly short of a micronutrient looks normal but grows and yields less. Soil surveys count these latent shortages too. By the time leaves show it, a part of the yield is already gone, so testing before the season is worth more than spraying after symptoms appear.

1565	Zinc and phosphorus: why heavy DAP can cause zinc deficiency
1566	Phosphorus and zinc compete. When a field gets heavy phosphate year after year, the crop can take up less zinc, and the soil's zinc becomes less available, especially in alkaline soils. The answer is not less phosphorus where the crop needs it, but balanced feeding: phosphorus by soil test, and zinc applied where the test shows it is short.
1567	<a href="#s1">Signs in a DAP-heavy field</a>
1568	High phosphate in the soil can bind zinc into forms roots cannot use.
1569	Inside the plant, very high phosphorus can reduce the zinc that reaches new leaves.
1570	Alkaline, calcareous soils make the problem worse.
1571	Signs in a DAP-heavy field
1572	Good early colour but small, narrow new leaves.
1573	Patchy growth even with plenty of NPK.
1574	Yields that stopped rising though fertilizer use keeps rising.
1575	Test the soil and apply phosphorus to the test, not by habit.
1576	Apply zinc separately from phosphate fertilizers, not mixed in the same bag.
1577	Use foliar zinc on the standing crop if new growth shows shortage.
1578	Build organic matter, which keeps both phosphorus and zinc available.
