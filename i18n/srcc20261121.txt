8	Castor (Erandi) guide
9	Castor (Erandi) stage by stage
10	Castor is a deep-rooted oilseed crop that stays in the field for many months, so root depth and soil health matter more than in a short crop. Growers bring us stunted plants on hard soil, wilting plants and spikes that do not fill well.
11	Main growing areas: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
12	Sowing and establishment
13	First 3 to 4 weeks
14	Loose soil and moisture let the taproot go deep and give an even stand.
15	Vegetative growth and branching
16	About 1 to 3 months
17	Strong roots and steady nutrition build the branches that carry the spikes.
18	Spike development
19	About 3 to 5 months
20	The main spike and later spikes set and fill. Water or nutrient stress now leaves gaps in the spike.
21	From about 4 to 5 months, in rounds
22	Capsules ripen spike by spike and are picked in several rounds.
23	Common castor (erandi) problems
24	Stunted plants on hard, compact soil
25	Wilting plants (wilt and root rot)
26	Pale or yellow leaves
27	Gaps and poor filling in spikes
28	Grey mould on spikes after wet weather
29	Loosen compacted soil before sowing so roots can go down.
30	Avoid waterlogging; castor roots rot in standing water.
31	Rotate with another crop where wilt has appeared.
32	Dig up a wilting plant and look at the root and collar; send us a photo.

36	Castor (Erandi) questions
37	Why is my castor wilting?
38	Wilt and root rot are common causes. Look at the root and the cut stem for browning, and check for waterlogging or a field where castor was grown before.
39	Can castor grow well on hard soil?
40	Castor roots are strong, but hard, compact soil still limits them. Loosening the soil and adding organic matter help the roots go deeper.

46	Tobacco guide
47	Tobacco stage by stage
48	Tobacco is grown for its leaf, so leaf size, weight and quality decide the crop. It is also sensitive to chloride, salty water and wet roots. Growers bring us slow early growth, small leaves and wilting plants.
49	Main growing areas: Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka.
50	Nursery
51	About 5 to 6 weeks
52	Healthy, even seedlings give an even field.
53	Transplanting and establishment
54	First 2 to 3 weeks
55	Moist soil at transplanting and quick root growth decide the start.
56	Rapid leaf growth
57	Leaf size and weight are built now. Steady moisture and balanced nutrition matter most.
58	Topping and ripening
59	From about 2 to 3 months
60	Topping, sucker control and leaf ripening decide quality before harvest and curing.
61	Common tobacco problems
62	Slow early growth after transplanting
63	Small, thin leaves
64	Wilting and root rot
65	Leaf spots
66	Leaf edge burn (chloride or salt)
67	Raise healthy seedlings and transplant into moist soil.
68	Use chloride-free potash and avoid salty irrigation water.
69	Avoid waterlogging and keep irrigation regular.
70	Rotate fields where wilt or nematodes have built up.
71	Test the soil and irrigation water.

73	Tobacco questions
74	Why are my tobacco leaves small?
75	Small leaves usually follow a slow start: weak roots, salty water, dry spells or low nutrition in the first 1 to 2 months.
76	Why do the leaf edges burn?
77	Chloride or salt in the soil or water burns leaf edges and harms leaf quality. Tobacco is a chloride-sensitive crop.
