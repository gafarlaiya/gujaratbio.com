## CROP GUIDES (general agronomy; stages and common problems)
### Sugarcane (grown in: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh; product: immunity-sugarcane-special)
Sugarcane stays in the field for a year or more, and the same land often grows it again and again. Over decades this wears the soil out. Growers then see poor germination, gaps in the row, uneven growth and weak ratoons, even with new varieties and more fertilizer.
Stages: Planting and germination (About the first 6 weeks): Setts need moist, airy soil and healthy buds. Poor germination, sett rot and gaps show up here. Gaps filled late never catch up. | Tillering (About 6 weeks to 4 months): The number of tillers decides the number of millable canes. Weak roots, compacted soil and early water stress reduce tillering. | Grand growth (About 4 to 9 months): Canes put on height and weight. The crop needs steady water and balanced nutrition. Lodging and pests matter most now. | Maturity and ratoon (About 9 months onward): Sugar builds up in the cane. After harvest, stubble care and soil health decide how good the ratoon will be.
Common problems: Poor germination and gaps; Plants dying after germination; Uneven growth; Weak ratoons; Soil hard and tired after years of sugarcane; More fertilizer every year for the same yield
What to check: Get a soil test: organic carbon, pH and salts (EC) tell you a lot about tired land.; Use healthy, fresh setts from a good nursery.; Look at the roots of weak plants: are they short, brown or few?; Note where gaps are: low spots and hard patches point to water and soil structure problems.
### Potato (grown in: Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Gujarat; product: immunity-potato-special)
Potato is a short, heavy-feeding crop. Many growers now use more fertilizer every season but get fewer tubers, smaller sizes and weaker skins. The cause is often below the ground: tired soil and weak roots at the moment tubers are forming.
Stages: Planting and emergence (About the first 3 to 4 weeks): Seed tubers sprout and roots start. Seed quality, soil moisture and soil temperature decide how evenly plants come up. | Vegetative growth (About 3 to 6 weeks): Leaves and stems build the plant's food factory. Strong roots now support more tubers later. | Tuber initiation (About 6 to 8 weeks): The plant decides how many tubers it will carry. Stress at this stage (water, heat, nutrient shortage) means fewer tubers. | Bulking and maturity (About 8 weeks to harvest): Tubers grow in size and weight, then the skin sets. Steady water and potash matter most.
Common problems: Fewer tubers per plant; Small or uneven tubers; Weak skin, poor keeping quality; Yields falling on the same land; Fertilizer use going up every season
What to check: Use certified, healthy seed tubers of the right size.; Test the soil before planting; potato land often has high salts or low organic carbon.; Avoid water stress at tuber initiation.; Dig a few plants at 7 to 8 weeks and count the tubers set.
### Mango (grown in: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka and across India; product: immunity-mango-special)
A mango tree is a long-term asset, and most problems build up over years. Growers bring us the same list: trees in decline, weak new growth, a good year followed by a poor one, flowers and fruit dropping, and small fruit.
Stages: After harvest (Monsoon): Pruning, cleaning and feeding the tree. New growth (flush) after harvest is next season's fruiting wood. | Before flowering (Autumn and early winter): The tree needs mature shoots and stored food to flower well. Heavy late flush or stress now reduces flowering. | Flowering and fruit set (Winter to early spring): Weather, pests and the tree's reserves decide how many fruits set. Some drop is natural; heavy drop is not. | Fruit growth (Spring to summer): Fruit size and quality need steady water and nutrition. Stress now causes late drop and small fruit.
Common problems: Trees weakening or dying; Poor flush after harvest; Alternate bearing; Heavy flower and fruit drop; Small fruit, poor colour and taste
What to check: Look at the root zone: is water standing, or is the soil hard and dry?; Check the trunk and branches for gummosis, borers and dieback.; Keep a record of each tree's yield year by year.; Test the soil every two to three years.
### Apple (grown in: Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir; product: immunity-apple-special)
Apple growers in the hills face trees dying in the orchard, low production and poor fruit, while costs rise every year. Many of these problems start in the root zone and build up over seasons.
Stages: Dormancy (Winter): Pruning, orchard cleaning and soil work. Chilling hours decide how well buds break in spring. | Bud break and flowering (Spring): Even bud break and good pollination set the crop. Frost and poor pollination reduce fruit set. | Fruit growth (Summer): Fruit size, colour and firmness build up. Water, balanced nutrition and leaf health matter most. | After harvest (Autumn): The tree stores food for next year. Leaf health after harvest matters for next season's buds.
Common problems: Trees dying in the orchard; Root rot and collar rot; Low production; Small, poorly coloured fruit; Rising input costs
What to check: Dig near a weak tree and look at the roots: healthy roots are white and firm, rotting roots are brown and soft.; Check drainage: water standing around trunks invites root rot.; Look at the collar (where trunk meets soil) for cankers.; Test the soil for pH and organic carbon.
### Banana (grown in: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka; product: immunity-banana-special)
Banana grows fast and feeds heavily. In Central Gujarat growers brought us wilt and leaf spot problems, plant losses and poor bunch weight. Soil health, clean planting material and balanced nutrition all matter.
Stages: Planting and establishment (About the first 2 to 3 months): Disease-free suckers or tissue-culture plants and good drainage set the plantation up. | Vegetative growth (About 3 to 7 months): The plant builds leaves; the number of healthy leaves decides bunch size later. | Shooting and bunch (About 7 to 10 months): The bunch emerges and fills. Healthy leaves, water and potash matter most. | Harvest (About 11 to 14 months): Bunch weight and finger quality depend on how the plant was fed all season.
Common problems: Wilt (Panama disease); Leaf spot (Sigatoka); Plants dying; Low bunch weight; Poor finger size and quality
What to check: Use disease-free planting material.; Look for yellowing that starts on older leaves and a split, discoloured stem, which point to wilt.; Remove badly spotted leaves.; Keep drainage good; banana hates standing water.
### Citrus (grown in: Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Punjab, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh; product: immunity-citrus-special)
Citrus orchards (lemon, lime, sweet orange, mandarin) often suffer from dieback, where branches dry from the tip back, along with fruit drop and declining trees. Roots and drainage are usually where the trouble starts.
Stages: Flowering (Depends on bahar (season)): Water stress followed by irrigation triggers flowering. Even flowering gives an even crop. | Fruit set (After flowering): Some drop is natural. Heavy drop follows stress or poor nutrition. | Fruit growth (Several months): Fruit size and juice need steady water and nutrition. | Rest (Between crops): Pruning dead wood and feeding the tree for the next bahar.
Common problems: Dieback of branches; Fruit drop; Yellowing leaves; Trees declining; Small fruit
What to check: Check drainage and look at the roots.; Look for gummosis at the trunk base.; Check for zinc and iron shortage: yellow leaves with green veins.; Test soil pH; citrus struggles in very alkaline soil.
### Coconut (grown in: Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and coastal Gujarat; product: immunity-coconut-special)
A coconut palm flowers every month, so its nutrition affects yield all year round. Low nut yield, button (young nut) shedding and weak palms are the problems growers ask about most.
Stages: Young palm (First 4 to 6 years): Root and trunk growth. Good feeding now brings earlier bearing. | Bearing palm (Year-round): A new spadix opens about every month. Nutrition today shows in nuts about a year later. | Nut development (About 12 months per bunch): Water and potash matter most for nut size and copra.
Common problems: Low nut yield; Button shedding; Small nuts; Yellowing fronds; Weak or declining palms
What to check: Count nuts per palm per year to track yield.; Check the root zone: basins should hold water without standing for days.; Look for pests like rhinoceros beetle and red palm weevil.; Test the soil every two to three years.
### Cotton (grown in: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab and Haryana; product: immunity-cotton-special)
Cotton yield depends on how many bolls the plant keeps. Early stress, weak roots and poor soil lead to square and boll shedding and a lower crop.
Stages: Sowing and seedling (About the first month): Even emergence and early root growth set the crop up. | Squaring (About 1 to 2 months): Flower buds (squares) form. Stress now causes shedding. | Flowering and boll set (About 2 to 3 months): The plant decides how many bolls it can carry. | Boll development (About 3 months to picking): Bolls fill and open. Water and potash matter most.
Common problems: Poor emergence; Weak early growth; Square and boll shedding; Reddening of leaves; Yields falling on the same land
What to check: Test soil before sowing.; Watch for sucking pests in early growth.; Avoid water stress at flowering.; Count bolls on a few plants to judge the crop.
### Onion (grown in: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Rajasthan; product: immunity-onion-special)
Onion has a shallow, weak root system, so it depends on soil that stays loose and moist. Growers bring us small or uneven bulbs, leaf tips drying early and onions that rot in storage.
Stages: Nursery and transplanting (About the first 6 to 8 weeks): Healthy, even seedlings and moist soil at transplanting give an even crop. | Leaf growth (About 1 to 2 months after transplanting): Each healthy leaf adds a ring to the bulb. Leaf loss now means smaller bulbs. | Bulbing (About 2 to 3 months): Bulbs swell. Steady water and potash matter most; stop excess nitrogen. | Maturity and curing (Harvest and after): Necks fall over. Good curing decides how long onions keep.
Common problems: Small or uneven bulbs; Leaf tips drying early; Weak, short roots; Bulbs splitting or doubling; Rotting in storage
What to check: Transplant healthy seedlings of even size.; Keep watering light and regular; avoid water stress at bulbing.; Stop irrigation before harvest so bulbs cure well.; Test the soil; onion dislikes salty soil.
### Pomegranate (grown in: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat and Rajasthan; product: immunity-pomegranate-special)
Pomegranate is grown by bahar (season), and how the plant is fed and watered decides flowering, fruit set and fruit quality. Growers bring us flower drop, cracked fruit and weak plants.
Stages: Rest and pruning (Before the bahar): Rest, pruning and feeding prepare the plant to flower. | Flowering (After the rest period): Even flowering gives an even crop. Stress causes flower drop. | Fruit growth (About 4 to 5 months): Steady water avoids cracking; balanced nutrition builds size and colour. | Harvest (End of the bahar): Fruit colour, size and shelf life reflect care through the season.
Common problems: Flower and fruit drop; Fruit cracking; Small fruit, poor colour; Weak plants; Plants dying
What to check: Keep soil moisture steady during fruit growth; sudden watering after dryness cracks fruit.; Prune and clean plants between bahars.; Check roots and stems of weak plants.; Test soil and water.
### Paddy (grown in: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Gujarat; product: immunity-paddy-special)
Paddy is grown in standing water, which changes how nutrients behave in the soil. Growers bring us weak tillering, yellowing after transplanting and chaffy, poorly filled grain.
Stages: Nursery and transplanting (About the first month): Healthy seedlings and quick recovery after transplanting set the crop up. | Tillering (About 1 to 2 months): The number of tillers decides the number of panicles. | Panicle and flowering (About 2 to 3 months): Stress now reduces grains per panicle. | Grain filling (Last month): Grain weight builds up; leaf health matters most.
Common problems: Weak tillering; Yellowing after transplanting; Zinc shortage (rusty brown spots); Chaffy, poorly filled grain; Lodging
What to check: Transplant young, healthy seedlings.; Watch for zinc shortage on alkaline soils.; Keep water levels right for the stage.; Count tillers per hill at mid-tillering.
### Maize (grown in: Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Bihar and Telangana; product: immunity-maize-special)
Maize grows fast and feeds heavily. Growers bring us weak early growth, yellow leaves and cobs with gaps or small size.
Stages: Emergence (First 2 weeks): Even emergence gives even plants and cobs. | Knee-high to tasselling (About 3 to 7 weeks): The plant decides how big the cob can be. | Silking and pollination (About 8 to 10 weeks): Water stress now causes gaps on the cob. | Grain filling (Until harvest): Grain weight builds up.
Common problems: Weak early growth; Yellowing leaves; Gaps on the cob; Small cobs; Stress in dry spells
What to check: Sow at the right time into moist soil.; Avoid water stress at silking.; Watch for fall armyworm early.; Test the soil before sowing.
### Tea (grown in: Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka; product: immunity-tea-special)
Tea bushes are plucked for decades, and the acidic soil they grow in wears out. Garden managers bring us slow flush, tired bushes and poor leaf quality.
Stages: Pruning and rest (Cold season): Pruning resets the bush; feeding prepares new growth. | First flush (Spring): Even, strong flush starts the season. | Main flushes (Rains): Steady nutrition keeps flush coming. | Autumn flush (Late season): The bush stores reserves for next year.
Common problems: Slow new flush; Tired, ageing bushes; Poor leaf quality; Acidic, worn-out soil
What to check: Test soil pH and organic carbon regularly.; Return prunings to the soil.; Keep drainage good.; Watch for pests like tea mosquito bug.
### Cumin (Jeera) (grown in: Gujarat and Rajasthan; product: immunity-cumin-special)
Cumin is a short winter crop and very sensitive to stress and disease. Growers in Gujarat and Rajasthan bring us patchy fields, plants wilting and low seed set.
Stages: Sowing and emergence (First 3 weeks): Even emergence on well-prepared soil. | Branching (About 3 to 6 weeks): More branches, more umbels. | Flowering and seed set (About 6 to 10 weeks): Cloudy, humid weather now brings blight. | Maturity (About 100 to 120 days): Seeds turn brown; harvest on time.
Common problems: Patchy, weak plants; Plants wilting and dying; Blight in cloudy weather; Low seed set; Poor seed quality
What to check: Avoid fields where wilt was severe last season.; Use treated, healthy seed.; Do not over-irrigate; cumin hates wet soil.; Watch the weather at flowering.
### Chilli (Mirchi) (grown in: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat; product: immunity-chilli-special)
Chilli is picked over a long season, so the plant must keep flowering and fruiting for months. Growers bring us flower drop, leaf curl, small fruit and plants that stop bearing early.
Stages: Nursery and transplanting (About the first 6 weeks): Healthy seedlings, free of virus, give a healthy crop. | Vegetative growth (About 1 to 2 months after transplanting): Strong branches carry more fruit later. | Flowering and fruit set (From about 2 months): Stress causes flower drop. | Picking (Several months): Steady feeding keeps the plant bearing.
Common problems: Flower and fruit drop; Leaf curl; Small fruit; Plants stop bearing early; Fruit rot
What to check: Raise seedlings under net to keep insects out.; Control thrips and whiteflies early; they spread leaf curl.; Keep watering steady.; Feed after each picking.
### Groundnut (Mungfali) (grown in: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka; product: immunity-mungfali-special)
Groundnut flowers above ground and sets pods below it, so loose soil and calcium in the pod zone matter. Growers bring us few pods, empty pods and yellow plants.
Stages: Sowing and emergence (First 2 weeks): Treated seed and good moisture give an even stand. | Flowering and pegging (About 3 to 6 weeks): Pegs need loose soil to enter. | Pod development (About 6 to 12 weeks): Calcium and water in the pod zone fill the pods. | Maturity (About 100 to 120 days): Harvest when most pods are mature.
Common problems: Few pegs and pods; Empty or shrivelled pods; Yellowing (iron or nitrogen shortage); Collar rot and wilting
What to check: Keep the soil loose at pegging.; Supply gypsum where advised by your agriculture office.; Avoid water stress at pod filling.; Use Rhizobium-treated seed.
### Wheat and cereals (grown in: Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Gujarat; product: immunity-dhanya-special)
Wheat, bajra, jowar and other cereals feed the country. Growers bring us weak tillering, yellowing and grain that does not fill well, often on soil that has grown the same crops for years.
Stages: Sowing and emergence (First 3 weeks): Timely sowing and good seed give an even stand. | Tillering (About 3 to 6 weeks): More healthy tillers, more ears. | Booting and flowering (About 2 to 3 months): Heat and water stress reduce grains per ear. | Grain filling (Last month): Grain weight builds up; late heat shortens it.
Common problems: Weak tillering; Yellowing; Zinc shortage; Poor grain filling; Lodging
What to check: Sow on time for your area.; Use a soil test to balance fertilizer.; Irrigate at crown root initiation and flowering.; Watch for rust in wheat.
### Pulses (grown in: Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka and Gujarat; product: immunity-pulses-special)
Tur, moong, urad, chana and other pulses fix their own nitrogen through root nodules, but only when the soil and roots are healthy. Growers bring us flower drop, few pods and yellow plants.
Stages: Sowing and emergence (First 2 weeks): Rhizobium-treated seed starts nodulation early. | Vegetative growth (About 3 to 6 weeks): Pink nodules on the roots show nitrogen fixing is working. | Flowering and pod set (Mid-season): Stress now causes flower drop. | Pod filling (Until harvest): Grain size builds up.
Common problems: Flower drop; Few pods; Weak nodulation; Yellowing; Wilt
What to check: Treat seed with Rhizobium.; Dig a plant at 4 to 5 weeks and look for pink nodules.; Avoid waterlogging.; Watch for pod borer at flowering.
### Vegetables (grown in: Across India; product: immunity-vegetable-special)
Tomato, brinjal, okra, cabbage, cauliflower, gourds and other vegetables are picked again and again, so the plant needs steady feeding. Growers bring us flower drop, small fruit and plants that stop bearing early.
